Hampi sits among enormous boulders on the Tungabhadra river in northern Karnataka. It was the capital of the Vijayanagara Empire, one of the largest cities in the world in the 15th and 16th centuries, and its ruins, spread across about 40 square kilometres, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Temples, royal enclosures, markets and water tanks lie scattered through banana fields and rocky hills. It rewards slow travel, early mornings and a bicycle.

## Best time to visit Hampi

October to February is ideal, with sunny days and cool mornings. March to May is extremely hot and exposed, with little shade between the ruins. June to September is warmer and humid with some rain, and the landscape turns green. The Hampi Utsav cultural festival is usually held around November, though dates change each year, so confirm with Karnataka tourism before booking. Whenever you go, start sightseeing at sunrise and take a break at midday.

## How to reach Hampi

Hampi village has no railway station or airport of its own. The nearest railhead is **Hosapete (Hospet)**, about 13 km away; local buses, autos and taxis connect it to Hampi in 30 to 40 minutes.

**By train:** Hosapete has direct trains from Bengaluru and Hubballi. Overnight trains from Bengaluru take about eight to ten hours.

**By road:** Bengaluru is roughly 340 km away. Overnight buses reach Hosapete in around eight hours. Goa and Hyderabad are both long drives, so most travellers connect through Hosapete.

**By air:** The nearest options are small regional airports in the Ballari area and larger ones at Hubballi and Bengaluru. Check which airlines currently operate to them, because regional schedules change often.

## Getting around

The sites are far apart, so you need wheels. Rent a **bicycle** for flat areas around the Sacred Centre, or an **electric scooter** for the wider area. Auto-rickshaws will do a full-day sightseeing loop for a negotiated price. From the parking area at Vittala Temple, electric buggies carry visitors the last stretch.

## Top things to see

**Virupaksha Temple** is an active temple and the heart of Hampi Bazaar. Visit early, before the crowds, and look for the camera-obscura effect in a dark corridor, where an inverted image of the tower is cast on the wall.

**Vittala Temple** has the famous stone chariot, which appears on the ₹50 note. The main hall's carved pillars are called the "musical pillars"; tapping them is no longer permitted, to protect the stone.

**The Royal Enclosure** includes the Lotus Mahal, the Elephant Stables, the Queen's Bath and the Mahanavami Dibba platform. Allow three hours, and carry water.

**Matanga Hill** is the best sunrise viewpoint, a short but steep climb on stone steps. Start in the dark with a torch. **Hemakuta Hill** is easier and good for sunset, with early temples and wide views.

**Anjanadri Hill**, across the river near Anegundi, is traditionally believed to be Hanuman's birthplace. The climb is around 500 steps; go early and avoid it midday in summer.

**Sanapur Lake** offers coracle rides, the round woven boats of the region. Agree on the fare and route first.

## What to eat

Many places in Hampi village have traditionally served vegetarian food only, near the temple area. Expect South Indian thalis, dosas, fresh juices and banana pancakes in backpacker cafés. Non-vegetarian food and alcohol are easier to find across the river or in Hosapete. Rooftop cafés are popular for sunset, but check hygiene and drink only bottled or filtered water.

## Where to stay

**Hampi Bazaar / Virupaksha area:** closest to the temple and sunrise spots. Simple guesthouses and homestays, many with rooftop restaurants.

**Virupapur Gadde (across the river):** a quiet, rural stretch with rice paddies, boulders and a relaxed backpacker scene. You cross by small boat or take the long way by road; check the crossing times, which can change with water levels.

**Hosapete:** modern hotels and easier connectivity, but about 30 minutes from the ruins.

Indicative prices: dorm beds and basic rooms ₹500–1,500; comfortable rooms ₹2,000–5,000; resort stays ₹6,000 and up. Rates jump around holidays and festivals, so book ahead in peak season.

## Indicative costs

Bicycle rental is typically ₹100–200 a day, scooters cost more, and sightseeing by auto may run from ₹1,000 to ₹2,000 for a day depending on the route. Most major monuments charge an Archaeological Survey of India entry fee, with foreign visitors paying much more than Indian citizens. The Vittala Temple ticket is often valid across several nearby monuments on the same day. Verify current prices on the ground.

## A 2-3 day Hampi itinerary

**Day 1: Sacred Centre.** Sunrise at Matanga Hill, breakfast in the bazaar, then Virupaksha Temple, the Hampi Bazaar street, Krishna Temple and Lakshmi Narasimha. Rest in the afternoon, then sunset on Hemakuta Hill.

**Day 2: Royal and Vittala circuit.** Start early at Vittala Temple, walk the river path to Vittala Bazaar and the King's Balance, then spend the late morning at the Royal Enclosure. Visit Hazara Rama Temple and the Queen's Bath in the afternoon.

**Day 3: Across the river.** Cross to Anegundi, climb Anjanadri Hill at sunrise, visit Sanapur Lake for a coracle ride, and spend the afternoon at a quiet café in Virupapur Gadde. Photography and a slow lunch are the point of this day.

## Practical tips

- Wear a hat and sturdy shoes; many monuments have uneven stone and no shade.
- Dress modestly at active temples, and remove footwear where required.
- Bring cash for small vendors; ATMs are limited in Hampi village.
- Keep valuables out of sight, and be cautious climbing boulders in the dark.
- Respect the monuments: do not climb on structures or write on stone.
