Jaipur, the capital of Rajasthan, is the most approachable city on the Golden Triangle. It is compact enough to see its headline sights in three days, and rich enough that many travellers stay longer. The old city, painted in a terracotta pink since the 1870s, holds palaces, observatories and bazaars; the hills around it carry some of India's best-preserved forts. Jaipur's walled city and the Jantar Mantar observatory are both UNESCO World Heritage sites.

## Best time to visit Jaipur

October to March is the best window. Days are warm and clear, evenings are cool, and December and January can feel properly cold after dark, so carry a jacket. April to June is very hot, with daytime temperatures commonly above 40°C; if you must travel then, sightsee at dawn and rest in the afternoon. July to September brings the monsoon, which turns the Aravalli hills green and fills Jal Mahal's lake, but also brings humidity and sudden showers. Big festival periods such as Diwali, and the Jaipur Literature Festival (usually January), push hotel prices up, so book early.

## How to reach Jaipur

**By air:** Jaipur International Airport (JAI) at Sanganer has regular flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata and other major cities. It is roughly 12 km from the old city.

**By train:** Jaipur Junction is well connected to Delhi, Agra, Udaipur, Jodhpur and Mumbai. Fast daytime trains from Delhi take about four to five hours.

**By road:** Delhi is about 270 km away, a drive of roughly five hours on the highway. Agra is about 240 km. Rajasthan State buses and private coaches run all day.

## Getting around

App-based cabs and autos are the easiest way to cover distances; agree on a fare or insist on the meter. For the walled city, walk: the main bazaars are close together. A full-day private car with driver is a comfortable way to do the forts, and costs are usually negotiated by the day, so compare two or three quotes.

## Top things to see

**Amer Fort** is the highlight. Arrive when it opens to beat the crowds and heat, and walk up rather than taking the elephant ride, which many visitors now avoid for animal-welfare reasons. Allow two to three hours.

**City Palace** is still partly the royal family's residence. The courtyards, textile galleries and the Peacock Gate are worth the ticket.

**Hawa Mahal**, the honeycomb facade, looks best in early morning light from the cafés opposite. Inside, the building is small, so most visitors spend 30 minutes.

**Jantar Mantar** is an 18th-century astronomical observatory with giant stone instruments, including the world's largest stone sundial. Hire a guide or use an audio guide; without one it is easy to miss what each instrument does.

**Nahargarh and Jaigarh** forts overlook the city. Nahargarh is the classic sunset spot. Jal Mahal, floating in Man Sagar Lake, is a photo stop rather than a visit, as the palace is not open to the public.

**Albert Hall Museum** is lovely when lit up in the evening, and **Anokhi Museum of Hand Printing** in Amer explains the block-printing craft Rajasthan is known for.

## What to eat

Try dal baati churma (lentils with baked wheat balls and sweet crumble), laal maas (a fiery mutton curry), pyaaz kachori for breakfast, and ghewar, a honeycomb sweet that is a monsoon-season speciality. For a cold lassi, the long-established shops near MI Road are a local favourite. Vegetarian food is outstanding and easy to find everywhere.

## Where to stay

**Near the old city (Pink City, Chandpole):** best for walkers and photographers; small heritage havelis and guesthouses with rooftop views. Expect noise and narrow lanes.

**C-Scheme, MI Road and Bani Park:** quieter, with good cafés, a wide range of mid-range hotels and easier access to taxis.

**Outskirts and Amer road:** large palace-style resorts for a special stay.

Indicative nightly prices: hostels and budget rooms ₹800–2,000; comfortable mid-range hotels ₹3,000–7,000; heritage and palace hotels ₹10,000 and up. Prices vary a lot by season, so check live rates.

## Indicative costs

Monument tickets for Indian citizens are generally modest (tens to a couple of hundred rupees), while foreign visitors pay several times more. Ask whether a combined ticket is on offer for multiple monuments. A budget traveller can manage on about ₹2,500–3,500 per day including a simple room; a comfortable mid-range trip is closer to ₹6,000–10,000 per day. Always check current ticket prices before you travel.

## A 3-day Jaipur itinerary

**Day 1: The walled city.** Start at Hawa Mahal at opening time, then visit City Palace and Jantar Mantar, which sit side by side. Lunch at a local thali place. In the afternoon, walk Johari Bazaar for jewellery and Bapu Bazaar for textiles and juttis. Evening tea on a rooftop café.

**Day 2: Forts.** Reach Amer Fort at opening. Stop at Jal Mahal on the way back, then see Jaigarh Fort and its giant cannon. Rest at midday. Finish at Nahargarh Fort for sunset and dinner with a view.

**Day 3: Crafts and culture.** Visit Albert Hall Museum, then take a block-printing workshop or visit the Anokhi Museum. Spend the afternoon shopping for textiles, blue pottery and lac bangles. Close with a village-style dinner and folk performance on the city's outskirts.

## Practical tips

- Dress modestly at temples, and carry sunscreen, a hat and water.
- Bargain politely in bazaars, but fix a price before buying.
- Beware "free" guides and drivers who take you to shops; they earn commission.
- If you have a fourth day, Ranthambore National Park, Pushkar or Ajmer are common add-ons.
